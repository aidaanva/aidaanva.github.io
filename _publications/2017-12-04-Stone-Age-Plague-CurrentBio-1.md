In this paper, we reconstructed the first 6 genomes of *Yersinia pestis* from Europe dating to the Late Neolithic and Early Bronze Age period, showing that plague was widespread during this time period in Eurasia. This highlighted that *Y. pestis* has been infecting human populations for at least 5000 years and predates the first historically documented plague pandemic in the 6th century by millenia. We demonstrated that all the genomes recovered from this time period form a single branch in the *Y. pestis* phylogeny, which appears to be extinct nowadays. Interpreting the phylogenetic pattern of *Y. pestis* together with the ancient human population genetics studies, we proposed a scenario for the introduction of plague into Europe linked to the expansion of steppe pastoralist around 5000 years ago associated to the Yamnaya culture and subsequent reintroduction to Central Eurasia with the resurgence of European-farmer like ancestry around 3000 years ago.
