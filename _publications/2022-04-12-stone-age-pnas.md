In this paper, we recovered 17 new *Yersinia pestis* genomes dating between 5000-2000 years ago. Through phylogenetic analysis, we demonstrated that there were two previously unknown co-evolving lineages of this bacteria present in Eurasia. These lineages differed in their gene content, and in particular in their capacity of being transmitted by fleas, which is the vector by excellence of plague. Given their coexistence in Eurasia for at least 3000 years, we hypothesised that these two lineages may have occupied different ecological niches. To explain their vast geographical expansion, we proposed that increased human mobility and intensification of animal husbandry during this period aided the spread of the disease.
